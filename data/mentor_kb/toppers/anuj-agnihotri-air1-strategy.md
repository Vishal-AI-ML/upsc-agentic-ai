# Anuj Agnihotri (AIR 1, CSE 2024) — Strategy Notes

> High-value mentor knowledge. Present as "AIR 1's approach"; never quote the raw interview, coaching names, or say it came from a transcript unless the student asks. Always add his own caveat: these are personal strategies — the student should chart their own path, not blindly copy.

## Profile & the big jump
- Rawatbhata (small-town Rajasthan), MBBS AIIMS Jodhpur, optional Medical Science.
- 1st attempt 948 (5 below cutoff -> DANICS reserve list). 2nd attempt marks DROPPED despite more prep (reached interview, missed final). 3rd attempt (given while in DANICS training) -> **AIR 1, 1071** — a massive Mains jump.
- Turning point: after 2nd attempt marks fell, he sat down for 1-2 days, honestly found his gaps (mainly Mains), and fixed exactly those.

## The core insight: CONTENT vs APPLICABILITY
- Problem in first 2 attempts: he HAD a ready knowledge base + rich value-added content, but couldn't **apply** it under exam pressure ("revise committees 2-3 days before exam" never works — names don't come under pressure).
- Fix: **at the moment you prepare** any value-add (committee, report, data, keyword, jargon), spend **5 min brainstorming right then** — where else / which paper can I use this? Build the interconnections at prep time, not exam time.
- Then in answer writing: (1) try to replicate that content under timed pressure; (2) self-evaluate honestly — "Did I actually write what I knew? If not, why not? How do I fix it?" This refinement drove the Mains jump.

## Answer writing (Mains craft)
- **Sustain quality Q1 -> Q20**: most aspirants' last 2-3 answers collapse in quality. Credit = intensive, timed answer-writing practice. Mantra heard from a topper: "if your 20th answer has the same quality as your 1st, your paper went great."
- **Point-based** (not paragraph): short points 1-1.5 lines, each substantiated with an example/data. 10-marker -> ~2 themes x 4 points + 2 way-forward initiatives = 8-10 points; 15-marker -> ~13-15 points. But don't be tight-jacketed — if question demand is met in 2 points, summarise with a diagram; don't panic about point count.
- **Crisp intro** (2-3 lines, not 4-5 point long intros — that ate time and reduced point count).
- **Highlighting**: circle data-sets, box good jargon/terminology, underline key terms — to catch examiner's eye.
- **Diagrams / flowcharts / maps** wherever appropriate — boost visual appeal and capture attention more than plain points.
- Don't over-force "way forward / need of the hour" in every answer — answer the demand, best-utilise time, keep it neat & clean over fancy.

## Paper-day approach
- Order: first 5 questions together (first impression), then jump to larger 11-20, then return to 6-10 when time is squeezed. Largely **chronological**, planned by the clock ("at X o'clock I must be on this question").
- **Never leave a page blank** — fill the whole booklet. For an unknown question, write generic-but-relatable theme content in minimum time (e.g. 15-marker in 7 min, save 4 min for better answers elsewhere).
- All 20 attempted in every attempt; this time the difference was sustained QUALITY, not quantity.

## Prelims
### CSAT (do NOT ignore)
- Was over-confident; 2023 CSAT was hard, passed borderline (+2). Lesson: UPSC can make any paper hard any year.
- Identify your strength (comprehension / logical reasoning / quant — quant if maths background). Practice **last 10 years PYQs** yourself, find your weak spots & tricks. **5-7 dedicated days** are enough for qualifying confidence. Resource: free YouTube marathon (11-12 hr) sessions; "go for the teacher, not the platform."

### GS
- Pattern changing (2023 was unorthodox). **Don't separate current vs static** — build interconnections while reading news; keep asking "how else can this be asked?"
- **PYQs = first thing**, last ~15 years, studied MULTIPLE times — "like a mystery thriller, every rewatch reveals something new." Research each option's back-story (why was this option framed?).
- **Newspaper** (his strength, read 6-7 yrs, treated as entertainment): don't make separate bulky newspaper notes. Spend ~1 month first just learning what to pick. Keep **Google open alongside** — immediately research any term/phenomenon/process that seems askable; add only a small point/data to EXISTING notes.
- **Environment (~10-15 Qs) + Science & Tech (~7-10 Qs)**: no fixed boundaries — be strategic + CURIOUS. Example: saw "RNF technology" in a mosquito-repellent ad -> Googled -> India's first patented chemical repellent (askable in Prelims). Use coaching marathon videos at 2x to pick themes, then Google each. Environment: Google/Wikipedia each national park/tiger reserve — note species, vegetation (UPSC does reverse-engineering); 5 min per item for **subconscious registration** (Prelims answer is in front of you — recognition matters, don't rote-memorise everything). Wildlife/animal-behaviour documentaries before sleep help.
- **Conventional subjects** conventionally: Polity = Laxmikanth; Economy = current-oriented (note budget items like Angel Tax, then self-research on Google); Geography = **NCERTs are the best**, mapping via YouTube + a habit of Googling every city/mountain on **Google Maps** (thousands of visits -> whole map registers, helps in Mains too).
- **History** (Prelims-tough, but his interest): went in-depth via **IGNOU MA History notes** (crisp, eGyanKosh — e.g. search "Cholas IGNOU notes", 10-11 pg PDF, verbatim jargon appears in PYQs) + **Tamil Nadu NCERT** (extensive) + **Spectrum** (modern) + a **self-made modern-history timeline** revised multiple times.
- **Mocks**: enriching — improve knowledge, confidence, elimination skill. Free tests on Telegram; take OMR printouts at home and self-analyse. Didn't need paid test series.

## Mains — sources & subject strategy
- **Anti tight-jacketed advice**: take suggestions from everyone, use your own intelligence, chart your own path. Unconventional worked for HIM (even drew answer angles from movies/web-series) — but conventional core (NCERT, PYQ, Laxmikanth, answer writing) is non-negotiable for all.
- **Government sources first** (his edge): Ministry **Annual Reports** (read first ~10 pages — Minister/Secretary editorial; or the 2-3 pg summary), govt sites (NDMA, CDRI for Disaster Mgmt), **Yojana**, and **Economic Survey** (best for data/graphs — e.g. this year's ultra-processed-food data links to Society, not just Economy).
- Magazines (Economist, India Today) only in free time — low cost-benefit, don't burden a beginner.
- **Identify your weakness**: he was ignoring Society in GS1 (writing layman-generic answers). Made Society a priority — clubbed **Society + Social Justice + related GS3** for thematic reading; used sociological concepts/jargon so answers looked well-researched. (Difference between a layman's answer and an aspirant's answer must show.)
- **Club overlapping topics** (e.g. Poverty spans GS1/Social Justice/GS3/Ethics; Geography + Disaster Mgmt together) to avoid duplicating effort and build natural interlinkages.
- **GS2**: memorise important Articles + their phrasing (~50-60 key ones; e.g. was confident on Attorney General article this year). Judgments = best value-add — use **Supreme Court Observer** site; quote judgments verbatim even in conclusions (e.g. St. Xavier's case, Dr. Chandrachud: "we should not just live and let live, we should help live and help live" for secularism).
- **IR**: right jargon; podcasts (ORF YouTube), Dr. Jaishankar speeches & PM quotes (verbatim in conclusions). PSIR-background candidates' answers show clear difference — match that with correct terminology.
- **Template (light)**: keep a brief ready intro per topic and outcome-oriented/futuristic conclusions — but never force a fixed intro onto a mismatched question (backfires).
- **Topper answer copies**: open **2-3 toppers side-by-side** (multiple tabs), first solve the paper yourself, then compare. Pick diagrams from them. Named copies he rated: **Medha Anand** (Ethics, well-structured, diagrams), **Siddharth Shukla** (History/Ethics), **Animesh Pradhan** (GS3 data — took it as inspiration, not an impossible challenge).
- **GS3**: heavily data-driven (Economy + Environment). Use good quality data, graphs, maps, diagrams, data-sets; strong need-of-hour / way-forward initiatives (govt sources prioritised). Learned a shorter data-citation style from topper copies (avoid long "According to NFHS-5..." phrasing bloating the answer).
- **GS4 (Ethics)**: his favourite. For each of ~60-70 syllabus keywords: build conceptual understanding (Google/YouTube), a long + short definition, enrich examples over time. Ethics = **applied ethics** — needs the MOST answer-writing practice. Case studies: bring uniqueness — health-background inputs, mythology parallels (Mahabharata/Ramayana/Rishis; e.g. Rishi Agastya intro for a 2023 work-life-balance case study), conclude "as a bureaucrat". Use schools of philosophy (existentialism, consequentialism, Aristotle's virtue ethics, religious ethics). On quotes: don't be rigid about verbatim — 80-90% familiarity is fine, **essence matters over form**; approach quote-questions as thesis -> antithesis -> relevance.
- **Essay**: was average; realised it wasn't his strength, so he did NOT attempt anything too unique — went safe/average template to secure average marks (avoids extreme low). Anecdotal & contemporary-relevance intros, mythology, and his science background (an arc: Biology -> Anatomy -> Physiology -> Pathology). Practice writing **2 essays in 3 hrs** (not 1.5 hr each — creativity numbs).

## Note-making
- His own notes were haphazard/unorganised; no single compiled GS notes — and admits lack of compiled OPTIONAL notes in attempt 1 was a big disadvantage (pre-exam revision pressure). Later made extensive optional notes.
- Advice: don't rote-build all notes day one — first UNDERSTAND the subject. Then filter consumed content into **one-pagers per topic in answer format** and compile those; your own written notes are easiest to revise and most replicable. Compile value-additions (Polity articles' vocabulary, Environment data) that actually need repeated revision.

## Self-study vs Delhi / test series
- Never shifted to Delhi to study — prepared at **home comfort + family support**; first set foot in a coaching hub only the day after his first interview. "**Self-evaluation is the best evaluation** — I am the best evaluator for myself" (he's very self-critical).
- Did NOT join a Mains test series. Instead: **5-10 questions daily**, evaluated immediately (prevents backlog + registers better). Near exam, **simulate full-length** (5 papers back-to-back, ~3 times, till ~2 weeks before) — physically replicate Mains conditions.
- No fixed test count — depends on when YOU feel confident (some need 1000 answers, some 50). The signal you're ready: even an unseen question -> you can write 2 well-structured pages calmly.
- Delhi IS right for those who lack a study environment/peer group at home. Decide after talking to seniors; weigh comfort/food/cost.
- **FOMO** (compilations/others writing daily in Delhi) — don't carry it. Use what gives YOU confidence.

## Interview
- Low earlier (171, 173). Weakness: had the answer but couldn't **articulate** it — blurted least-important point first. Fix: **simulate pressure**, self-Q&A, friends' mock interviews at night, articulate in a time-bound way; today you can even practise with **AI**.
- Be **calm, natural, honest** — don't force fancy or politically-correct answers; give the answer you actually believe, backed by a solid argument. **Maintain a genuine smile.** Prepare your DAF/background hard (education, work exp, home = deterministic questions).
- Mocks (retired-bureaucrat panels) help remove fear — take negative feedback positively. Ignore "riddle/puzzle interview" reels — that's a **myth**; real interview is professional and linked to your profile/subjects.
- His final board (Sheelvardhan Singh) was very cordial. Sample Qs: "as a doctor & bureaucrat, what surgery + instruments would you choose?" -> answered "a **social surgery**, instruments = government initiatives, schemes & administration." Mostly medical-background + COVID-handling + geopolitics. 1st-attempt mistake: named home district for posting -> should say "ready to serve any part of the country."

## Habits, mindset & final message
- Cut off social media (Instagram/Facebook/WhatsApp) completely in first 2 attempts; used YouTube + Telegram (study) and Twitter (current affairs) extensively.
- Timetable flexes (esp. during service training) — obsess over **consistency + quality hours**, not a rigid schedule; never a fully-zero-study day.
- **Working aspirants**: don't quit your job before Prelims (Prelims is now unpredictable/complex — quitting adds burden). Keep the Prelims-to-Mains window free.
- **Marginalised backgrounds**: huge free resources (Telegram, YouTube, coaching free content) + state UPSC-prep schemes — use them.
- Keep a **Plan B / exit strategy** (his = medical background) as a safety net — UPSC is a chakravyuh with a big luck factor; know your attempt limit and protect your most productive years.
- **Mental health**: acknowledge low phases, talk to your support system, normalise it ("as a doctor, we should talk about it"). Don't tie happiness solely to UPSC — be happy with what you have. Be determined, dedicated, disciplined, motivated — and smiling.
